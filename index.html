Why service-area businesses get stuck

The hidden-address pin

Google says to hide your address if customers don't come to you. The location behind it still counts, so Maps tends to show you to the neighborhoods around your house, not across your whole area.

Thin website

A one-page site with "we come to you!" gives Google nothing to match against "ceramic coating Lakewood" or "mobile brake repair near me." Each service needs its own real page.

Shortcuts that get you suspended

Stuffing city names into the business name, listing a virtual office, or making a second profile at a friend's address can get the profile suspended. Many mobile owners try one of these.

Google's rules for businesses without a storefront

Summarized from Google's Business Profile guidelines, checked November 2026.

✓
One service-area profile for a business that travels to customers, with the address hidden.
✓
A service area of about two hours' drive from your base, as a general guide.
✓
Separate profiles only for genuinely separate locations, each with its own team.
✗
PO boxes or remote mailboxes as your address.
✗
Virtual offices unless staffed during business hours.
✗
Extra words in your business name: cities, services, taglines, phone numbers.

Questions from mobile business owners

Can a mobile business without a storefront have a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google lets a business that travels to customers create a service-area Business Profile. If customers don't come to your address, Google says to hide it and set a service area instead.

How big can my service area be on Google?

Google's guidelines say a service area generally shouldn't extend farther than about two hours of driving from where your business is based, though larger areas can be appropriate for some businesses.

Can I use a PO box or virtual office as my address?

No. Google does not accept PO boxes or remote mailboxes, and a virtual office is not eligible unless it is staffed during business hours.

Why do I only show up on Google Maps near my house?

Proximity is a strong local ranking signal, and a hidden address still has a location behind it. Showing up farther out depends on relevance and prominence: the right primary category, a website with a real page for each service, reviews that mention the work and the towns, and consistent listings elsewhere.

Should I add city names to my business name on Google?

No. Google only allows your real-world business name. Adding cities, services or taglines can get the profile suspended.
